Our Story

From Fish Farm to Reptile Paradise

A 50-Year Legacy

Colorado Gators began in 1977 as a fish farm. In 1987, the first alligators were brought in as "garbage disposals" — and the rest is history.

Today, we're home to hundreds of rescued reptiles including alligators, crocodiles, caimans, tortoises, turtles, snakes, and iguanas — all thriving in the warmth of our 2,050-foot deep geothermal well (87°F year-round).

We're not a zoo. We're a rescue. Every animal here has a story. Our mission is to provide a forever home for reptiles that no one else can or will take.

Meet the Family

Our Rescued Residents

Every reptile at Colorado Gators is a rescue — found, abandoned, or seized. We give them a warm, safe place to live out their lives.

Albino Alligators
Extremely rare genetic rarity — fewer than 1 in 100 alligators are born with albinism. A Colorado Gators specialty.
Extremely Rare
Morris — The Movie Star
Star of "Happy Gilmore" and "The Waterboy." Morris became a cultural icon before his passing — now preserved as taxidermy at the farm.
Celebrity
Crocodiles & Caiman
Multiple species of crocodiles and South American caimans roam the warm ponds alongside their alligator cousins.
Diverse Species
Tropical Birds
Meet Tuka the cockatoo and our vibrant macaws. Colorful African Grey parrots complete the flock — a bright addition to the farm.

Hands-On Experiences

Don't Just Watch — Get Up Close

Colorado Gators offers unique interactive experiences that bring you face-to-face with these incredible creatures.

Pet an Alligator
Hold and pet a juvenile alligator with hands-on supervision from our knowledgeable staff. A truly unforgettable experience for all ages.
Feed the Gators
Buy gator chow and personally feed the alligators as they come up from the water. Watch these ancient predators in action.
Photo Opportunities
Snap pics with albino gators, massive tortoises, and even a piece of movie history — Morris, the famous "Happy Gilmore" alligator.
Pet-Friendly Tour
Bring your pup! Colorado Gators welcomes well-behaved dogs. Piper the dog once visited and approved — though the African tortoises had other ideas.
